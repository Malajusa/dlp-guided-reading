# Authoritative Alpha-Epsilon Profiles

These are teacher-assessed placements. They are not suggestions and must not be neutralised, reassessed, or replaced by model-generated groups.

| Day | Group | Teacher-facing assessed profile | Default visual support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Alpha | Very low | Strongest purposeful support |
| Tuesday | Beta | Low | Substantial support |
| Wednesday | Gamma | At level | Moderate, selective support |
| Thursday | Delta | Above level | Minimal, strategic support |
| Friday | Epsilon | Year 9 reading level | None, except necessary scientific or technical diagrams |

Student materials display only the Greek group name. The assessed profile appears on the teacher sheet and in internal planning records.

## Common rule

All five groups receive the same age-appropriate Year 4/5 curriculum idea, current genre, essential factual integrity, and broad conceptual destination. Complexity changes through a genuine rewrite, not by deleting meaning from the lower groups or adding trivia to the higher groups.

Teacher-supplied point-of-need data may refine the default controls below. It does not change the assessed placement unless the teacher explicitly says so.

## Alpha - very low

- Prioritise accurate word recognition, supported fluency, and successful construction of meaning from connected text.
- Control unfamiliar grapheme patterns and the number of new multisyllabic words; pre-teach high-value morphology.
- Prefer short syntactic units, clear clause relationships, explicit connectives, stated referents, and one main idea at a time.
- Repeat essential vocabulary naturally and supply indispensable background knowledge within the passage or teacher introduction.
- Use near, explicit evidence for early inference and model how the clue supports the conclusion.
- Use strong, age-respectful visuals for setting, sequence, vocabulary, or concrete concepts. Do not let visuals reveal a target answer.
- Keep response demands cognitively worthwhile; oral rehearsal, locating, explaining, and supported evidence use are appropriate.

## Beta - low

- Include selected unfamiliar multisyllabic and multimorphemic words with planned decoding or morphology support.
- Use simple, compound, and short complex sentences with mostly explicit cohesion.
- Increase information per paragraph gradually while retaining clear topic continuity.
- Require students to connect nearby clues, track straightforward pronouns, and explain evidence with prompts available.
- Use substantial visuals, but remove support that merely decorates or duplicates every sentence.
- Expect a short evidence-based oral or written response with scaffolded justification where needed.

## Gamma - at level

- Target the supplied Year 4/5 English achievement demands.
- Use varied sentence structures, some unfamiliar technical or literary vocabulary, meaningful paragraphing, and genre-appropriate navigation features.
- Require literal and implied meaning, tracking across paragraphs, summarising, and explanation of structure or language choices.
- Use moderate, selective visuals that extend or clarify rather than restate the prose.
- Expect independent evidence location and a complete explanation at year-level depth.

## Delta - above level

- Increase syntactic density, vocabulary precision or abstraction, less explicit cohesion, information packing, and structural variation.
- Increase inference distance and require evidence integration across sections.
- Permit subtle motivation, qualified claims, or more than one plausible interpretation where the text supports it.
- Use minimal visuals only when they materially extend meaning, supply data, or support navigation.
- Require comparison, justification, evaluation, or synthesis without merely increasing response length.

## Epsilon - Year 9 reading level

- Preserve age-appropriate Year 4/5 subject matter while using Year 9 reading complexity.
- Use sophisticated syntax, dense cohesion, nuanced or abstract vocabulary, implicit relationships, ambiguity, competing perspectives, and distal evidence where appropriate.
- Expect evaluation of credibility or authorial choices, synthesis across the text, and precise evidence-based justification.
- Do not use decorative, atmospheric, character, or narrative illustrations.
- Permit only a functional scientific or technical diagram necessary for comprehension. Declare its page, type, purpose, and source in the manifest; do not use it to lower the intended prose demand.

## Non-examples

- Five texts that differ mainly in word count.
- Alpha receiving childish content while Epsilon receives the worthwhile idea.
- Beta and Gamma receiving essentially identical passages.
- Epsilon receiving Year 9 topics rather than Year 9 reading complexity.
- Increasing difficulty only by inserting unexplained technical vocabulary.
- Asking the same question verbatim when the text evidence and intended response depth differ.
