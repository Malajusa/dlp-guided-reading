# Year 4/5 Curriculum and Annual Reading Map

Use this reference to select and check content. It is a concise working map of the curriculum documents supplied for English, HASS, Health and Physical Education, and Design and Technologies. Recheck the authoritative curriculum documents when exact wording or current codes matter.

## Reading-first hierarchy

1. Teacher assessment and the intended reading move.
2. English reading and viewing demands.
3. Writing genre during Weeks 1-6.
4. HASS, Health, or Design and Technologies content.

Do not make a passage harder merely by adding facts or technical words. Do not weaken narrative coherence to display curriculum coverage.

## Writing genre by term

The genre connection applies in Weeks 1-6 only.

## English reading demands

### Year 4

Texts may entertain, inform, or persuade. Build literal and implied meaning; connect ideas across texts; describe how setting, character, and events develop ideas; recognise characteristic structures; and examine how language, literary devices, and visuals shape meaning. Use varied sentences, unfamiliar multisyllabic and multimorphemic words, purposeful text navigation, and comprehension strategies including predicting, visualising, connecting, monitoring, questioning, and summarising.

### Year 5

Extend into less familiar topics, purposeful skimming and scanning, evaluation of information and ideas, historical/social/cultural context, point of view, authorial choice, figurative language, technical vocabulary, complex event sequences, ethical dilemmas, cross-text checking, and evidence-based interpretation.

## HASS term spine

### Term 1: Civics and Citizenship

- Year 4: local government services; differences between rules and laws; groups, belonging, and identity.
- Year 5: democracy and electoral processes; roles of electors and representatives; laws, citizens and human rights; civic goals and shared values.

In narrative, let community rules, services, representation, belonging, or a collective decision shape the setting or problem. In other genres, compare viewpoints and evidence without turning the reading into a civics worksheet.

### Term 2: History

- Year 4: First Peoples' enduring connections to Country/Place and pre-contact life; the First Fleet; contact and colonisation impacts; world navigators, explorers, or traders to the late eighteenth century.
- Year 5: reasons for British colonies after 1800; effects of colonial settlement on environments and daily lives; experiences of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, convicts, and free settlers; significant people or groups in the Swan River Colony.

Treat sources, perspectives, cause and effect, continuity and change, and historical significance carefully. Do not manufacture false balance around rights, dispossession, or documented harm.

### Term 3: Geography

- Year 4: Africa and Europe; climates, vegetation, landforms, and native animals; environmental importance and protection; renewable and non-renewable resources; sustainable management including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander practices.
- Year 5: North and South America; ways people alter Australian places; bushfire, drought, cyclone, flood, and community responses.

Information reports may make this content explicit through classification, description, technical vocabulary, maps, diagrams, and cause-effect relationships.

### Term 4: Economics and Business

- Year 5 primary alignment: needs and wants; natural, human, and capital resources; sustainable goods and services; scarcity and trade-offs.
- Year 4 bridge: resource sustainability, community services, community needs, and familiar choices. Do not claim a formal Year 4 Economics and Business code when it is not present in the supplied HASS curriculum.

Narrative problems may arise from scarce resources, competing needs, production choices, exchange, sustainability, or consequences of decisions.

## Health content pool

Year 4/5 reading contexts may draw on resilience and persistence; emotions and positive responses; inclusion, respect and empathy; relationships; protective behaviours and help-seeking; reliable health messages; online and offline safety; healthy choices; physical activity and wellbeing; fair play, roles, rules, and cooperation.

Puberty, consent, private-body safety, abuse, and explicit harmful online scenarios are opt-in only. Teacher selection must be recorded before use.

## Design and Technologies content pool

The supplied Technologies document covers Design and Technologies, not Digital Technologies.

Year 4/5 contexts may include:

- forces, movement, sound, or light in products and systems;
- properties and suitability of materials and components;
- food and fibre production across cultures, times, and environments;
- food properties, preparation systems, and food safety;
- sustainability, regeneration, reuse, repurposing, and recycling;
- community needs and design occupations;
- design briefs, resources, labelled drawings, technical terms, sequences, competing factors, iteration, and evaluation against criteria.

Use a procedure only when it serves the reading and genre plan. Technologies passages should still provide connected, worthwhile reading rather than isolated instructions.
