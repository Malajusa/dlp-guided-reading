# Year 4/5 Guided Reading — 40-Week Overview

This overview is the authoritative weekly instructional spine for Shared and Guided Reading unless the teacher explicitly changes the sequence. Curriculum contexts, genres, assessment evidence, student interests, and group scaffolds serve the prescribed weekly reading focus; they do not silently replace it.

## Fixed weekly rotation

- Monday — Alpha
- Tuesday — Beta
- Wednesday — Gamma
- Thursday — Delta
- Friday — Epsilon

The reading focus, Learning Intention, and broad conceptual destination are common across all five groups. Text complexity, evidence distance, vocabulary, syntax, scaffolding, response sophistication, and visual support vary according to the teacher-assigned group profile.

## Whole-year progression principles

- Treat the sequence as a spiral curriculum. Once a comprehension process is taught, continue to revisit and integrate it in later weeks.
- Increase evidence distance deliberately across the year: from explicit or nearby evidence towards connections across paragraphs, sections, texts, and viewpoints.
- Every week may include literal and inferential comprehension. The nominated reading focus identifies the explicit teaching emphasis, not the only question type students encounter.
- Increase difficulty through evidence selection, inference distance, synthesis, competing plausible interpretations, vocabulary precision, syntax, cohesion, and authorial reasoning — not obscure vocabulary or word count alone.
- Open responses increasingly require students to explain why an answer is defensible and identify supporting evidence.
- Visual literacy is integrated where useful; images, diagrams, maps, tables, captions, symbols, and layout may contribute meaning rather than serve as decoration.
- Week 10 in each term is a mixed-comprehension diagnostic and application week. Use the resulting evidence to refine emphasis, scaffolding, question design, and teacher modelling in subsequent weeks while preserving this overview unless the teacher explicitly changes the sequence.
- During Weeks 1–6, connect reading to the current Writing genre where useful without turning Guided Reading into a writing lesson.
- Student interests may influence the selected topic or context only after the reading focus, curriculum, age-appropriateness, factual integrity, and instructional need are satisfied.

## Term 1 — Building evidence-based comprehension

Priority: establish the habits students need for later reading — locate, connect, explain, and justify. Evidence is predominantly within a sentence or nearby paragraph early in the term, then increasingly requires connections across paragraphs.

| Week | Reading focus | Learning Intention | Success Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Explicit information | Locate information stated directly in a text. | I can find the exact part of the text that answers a question and show my evidence. |
| 2 | Main idea and supporting details | Identify what a paragraph is mainly about and distinguish supporting information. | I can state the main idea and select details that genuinely support it. |
| 3 | Sequence and chronology | Track the order of events, ideas or steps. | I can reconstruct a sequence and explain how I know the correct order. |
| 4 | Cause and effect | Explain why something happened and what resulted from it. | I can identify a cause and its effect using evidence from the text. |
| 5 | Vocabulary in context | Work out the meaning of unfamiliar words using surrounding information. | I can propose a word meaning and identify the contextual clues that support it. |
| 6 | Cohesion and reference | Track pronouns and other referring words across sentences. | I can explain what words such as *it, they, this, these* and *which* refer to. |
| 7 | Summarising | Condense important information without copying unnecessary detail. | I can retain the central meaning while removing examples and repetition. |
| 8 | Prediction from evidence | Make justified predictions rather than guesses. | I can predict what is likely to happen and cite clues that make the prediction reasonable. |
| 9 | Introductory inference | Combine stated clues with reasonable background knowledge. | I can explain an unstated idea and show which clues led me to it. |
| 10 | Mixed comprehension and diagnostic | Select the appropriate comprehension process independently. | I can answer mixed retrieval, vocabulary, main-idea and inference questions and justify my answers. |

## Term 2 — Purpose, persuasion and viewpoint

Priority: complement Persuasive Writing during Weeks 1–6 while maintaining reading as the instructional focus.

| Week | Reading focus | Learning Intention | Success Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Purpose and audience | Determine why a text was created and who it is intended for. | I can identify purpose and audience using features of the text as evidence. |
| 2 | Claims, reasons and evidence | Distinguish what an author wants us to believe from the support they provide. | I can identify a claim and link it to relevant reasons or evidence. |
| 3 | Fact and opinion | Distinguish verifiable information from judgement or belief. | I can classify statements and explain the evidence for my classification. |
| 4 | Persuasive language | Analyse how wording influences a reader. | I can identify an influential language choice and explain its intended effect. |
| 5 | Modality and certainty | Interpret how strongly an author presents a claim. | I can compare language expressing possibility, probability and certainty and explain its effect. |
| 6 | Persuasive structure | Explain how information is organised to influence the reader. | I can explain how an introduction, reasons, evidence and conclusion work together. |
| 7 | Author viewpoint | Infer an author's position when it is not stated in one sentence. | I can combine language and content clues to identify the author's viewpoint. |
| 8 | Text and visual meaning | Interpret how images, symbols, captions and layout contribute to a message. | I can explain what a visual adds rather than merely describe what it shows. |
| 9 | Comparing viewpoints | Compare how two authors treat the same subject. | I can identify agreement and disagreement and provide evidence from both texts. |
| 10 | Mixed comprehension and diagnostic | Apply purpose, persuasion, inference and comparison skills to unfamiliar texts. | I can choose evidence from different parts of one or more texts to justify answers. |

## Term 3 — Reading information critically

Priority: align naturally with Information Report writing while extending students beyond simply finding facts.

| Week | Reading focus | Learning Intention | Success Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Information text structure | Use headings, paragraphs and organisational features to navigate information. | I can explain how a text is organised and use its structure to find information efficiently. |
| 2 | Classification and relationships | Recognise how information is grouped into categories and subcategories. | I can identify the organising categories and explain relationships between information. |
| 3 | Technical vocabulary and morphology | Use word parts and context to interpret specialised vocabulary. | I can use roots, prefixes, suffixes and context to explain an unfamiliar word. |
| 4 | Relevant versus irrelevant detail | Decide which information actually answers a question or supports an idea. | I can reject plausible but irrelevant details and select the strongest evidence. |
| 5 | Reading diagrams, maps and tables | Integrate written and visual information. | I can obtain information from a visual and combine it with information from the prose. |
| 6 | Explaining cause and effect | Trace multi-step causal relationships in factual texts. | I can explain how one factor leads to another using evidence across the text. |
| 7 | Synthesising across paragraphs | Combine separate pieces of information to form a complete answer. | I can locate evidence in more than one section and combine it accurately. |
| 8 | Comparing information texts | Compare how two texts present information about a common topic. | I can identify shared information, differences and information unique to each source. |
| 9 | Reliability and credibility | Evaluate whether information deserves confidence. | I can use authorship, evidence, purpose and consistency to make a supported judgement about reliability. |
| 10 | Mixed comprehension and diagnostic | Solve unfamiliar factual-reading problems independently. | I can retrieve, infer, synthesise and evaluate information across prose and visuals. |

## Term 4 — Literary interpretation and advanced comprehension

Priority: apply the year's evidence habits to longer inference chains, authorial choices, multiple interpretations, and comparison.

| Week | Reading focus | Learning Intention | Success Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Narrative structure and plot | Explain how events build and change a narrative. | I can identify important turning points and explain why they matter to the plot. |
| 2 | Characterisation | Infer character traits, feelings and motives from behaviour and language. | I can make a character inference and support it with multiple clues. |
| 3 | Character change | Track how and why a character's attitude, relationship or behaviour changes. | I can compare a character at different points and explain what caused the change. |
| 4 | Setting and mood | Analyse how setting contributes to atmosphere, events and reader expectations. | I can identify specific setting details and explain their effect. |
| 5 | Figurative and precise language | Interpret language beyond its literal meaning. | I can explain what an image or expression suggests and why the author selected it. |
| 6 | Narrator and point of view | Explain how perspective shapes what the reader knows and understands. | I can identify the viewpoint and explain how it affects interpretation. |
| 7 | Theme and implied message | Infer a broader idea developed through characters and events. | I can express a theme as an idea and support it with evidence from different parts of the text. |
| 8 | Complex inference | Combine distant or subtle clues and manage ambiguity. | I can construct an inference chain and distinguish evidence from assumption. |
| 9 | Comparing texts and interpretations | Synthesise ideas across two related texts. | I can compare themes, viewpoints or authorial choices using precise evidence from both texts. |
| 10 | Independent mixed reading challenge | Integrate the year's reading processes without being told which strategy to use. | I can determine what a question requires, locate the strongest evidence and justify a defensible interpretation. |
